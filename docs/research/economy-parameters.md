# Economy parameters (fresh-game fieldwork, round 3)

Issue #3 (T-01). One fresh game of the original 1991 game in the browser emulator, played to Week 9, plus a few readings from an abandoned first attempt. This is the evidence for the balance pass (T-15) and for re-issuing the v1 spec. It does not change any code.

Confidence tags: **high** (read directly from a screen, repeated), **medium** (read directly once, or several readings that scatter), **low** (inferred, or one noisy reading). `?` means not observed.

## How this was played and measured

- Run A (counted): 1 player, no Jones ("challenge Jones?" answered no), Goal points left at the default 200, start $200. Played Week 1 to Week 9. All numbers below come from this run unless marked "run B" or "R2" (round 2, `original-game-fieldwork.md`).
- Run B (abandoned in Week 1): the same setup with Wealth moved up one step (Goal points 210). The tab was navigated away by another session, so the game was lost. Only a few readings from it are used and each is marked.
- Statistics screen (new): right-click the middle of the Board. It lists job and wage, Cash, Savings, Rent Owed, Loan Balance, Total Goods, Investment Total, Net Worth, then the lists GOODS, EDUCATION and INVESTMENTS. Cash is also on the calculator at the bottom right while a shop screen is open. I found no screen that shows Goal progress or happiness.
- Time: the dial has no numbers. I counted red pixels in a zoomed capture of the dial and expressed every cost as a percentage of a full dial. A full dial reads 0.9472 on my pixel scale (this was the value every time the Week was used up), so cost % = difference / 0.9472. A single reading is good to about 1 to 2 points. The pointer arrow can hide part of the dial, so I moved it away and waited 2 seconds before each reading. Readings taken before a long walk finished were discarded.
- Click gotchas: a walk is started by clicking the door or lower part of a building (the Employment Office needs a click near the bottom of its picture; the sign does nothing). A long walk takes 10 to 15 seconds in the emulator and the arrival screen only opens at the end. A shopkeeper bubble does not block clicks: the click goes through to the menu item behind it (I bought food by accident this way). After a "Your Landlord garnished" bubble, click the picture panel to dismiss it; the calculator updates only after that.

Reading:

- Cost grows with distance but scatters by about plus or minus 30% for the same distance, and a walk and its reverse can differ (Black's Market and Monolith: 9.0 against 16.0). Two legs added together can cost less than the direct walk, so cost is not a sum along the ring. I could not find the rule. A table keyed by distance fits the means: about 3 to 4% for 1, 6 to 7% for 2 to 3, about 7 to 8% for 4, about 9 to 10% for 5 (interpolated, not measured), about 11.6% for 6 to 7 (the mean of my three 7-step walks is 11.6, equal to R2's "long" value).
- Hungry Weeks: the two paired walks in the hungry Week 4 cost about 1.45 times the same walks in fed Weeks 3 and 5 (4.6 against 3.2, 11.7 against 7.9). Work was not affected (9.5% a click in Week 4). R2 measured everything in hungry Weeks (3 to 7), so its walking table is probably 15 to 20% too high. Low-medium confidence: two pairs only.
- Black's Market to Employment Office in Week 1 read 15.3%. I had clicked the Employment Office three times without effect before the walk started; those clicks may have cost time. Ignore that reading.
- Week fit: a Week holds about 26 one-step walks (R2) or, with no walking, about 9 work clicks. A typical Week of Monolith work is one 3 to 6% walk and 9 clicks.

## 6. Prices and wages move together (DEC-021, DEC-024)

Price level in my run (Week 1 = 1.00), from Black's Market food, Monolith Fries and the Rent Office list, which agree with each other to about 1%:

| Week  | 1    | 2         | 3    | 4    | 5    | 6    | 7    | 8    |
| ----- | ---- | --------- | ---- | ---- | ---- | ---- | ---- | ---- |
| Level | 1.00 | about 1.0 | 1.04 | 1.02 | 1.00 | 0.92 | 0.89 | 0.91 |

- The moves are small and look random: +4%, -2%, -2%, -8%, -3%, +2%. This run stayed between 0.89 and 1.04. With R2 the seen range is 0.83 to 1.22. Confidence: high that the level is shared by all shop prices and the Hi-Tech U fee ($50 in W1, $52 in W3, +4% like food); medium that the range has not been fully seen.
- Wages on the Employment Office lists follow the same level. Black's Market in Week 1 / Week 3 / Week 9: Janitor $5 / $5 / $4, Checker $8 / $8 / $7, Butcher $12 / $12 / $10, Assistant Manager $15 / $15 / $13, Manager $18 / $18 / $16. R2's Week 3 and 7 lists (level about 1.20 and 1.22) were about 15 to 20% above my Week 1 lists, and the two R2 lists look the same because 1.20 and 1.22 round to nearly the same dollars. My Week 9 lists were about 0.87 of Week 1 (I did not read the level in Week 9; Week 8 was 0.91). **This contradicts "Wages on the Employment Office lists did not follow" (DEC-021) and "wages are fixed" (DEC-024).** Confidence: high that listed wages move with the level, medium on the rounding (whole dollars; both rounding down and to nearest fit different entries).

## Proposed spec changes (for the author to re-issue; this PR does not edit the spec)

1. DEC-021: remove "Wages ... did not follow it". Replace with: wages on the Employment Office list scale with the level; the pay of a job you hold is fixed at the wage when hired.
2. DEC-024: split the rule into two data rules, shop prices follow the level, listed wages follow the level (whole dollars, rounding to be chosen), held pay fixed. AC-010 and AC-011: "wage not inflation-adjusted by default" becomes "a held job's wage is not adjusted".
3. DEC-025: keep the table keyed by distance, set distance 4 to about 7.5% and 5 to about 9%, and note that the original scatters by plus or minus 30% around the table and costs about 1.45 times more in a hungry Week. The balance pass may use the means and no scatter.
4.
